# Philosophy of the Research Wiki

This template is designed to reduce a familiar failure mode in research workflows: a plausible but unverified detail enters the wiki, gets repeated across notes, and eventually becomes a citation that looks true but is not.

## Accuracy-first design

The system prioritizes verification over speed. Every note should be defensible against a primary source. If the evidence is not there, the answer is to leave the gap empty rather than fill it with a likely story.

## Why the five-layer structure matters

A research wiki becomes unreliable when source summaries, concept synthesis, and project interpretation are mixed together in one page. The five-layer model keeps each voice distinct:

- Layer 0: raw sources
- Layer 1: literature notes
- Layer 2: concept notes
- Layer 3: atomic claims
- Layer 4: project hubs
- Layer 5: indexes and navigation

This makes it easier to verify what was said by the author, what the field says, and what the researcher is interpreting.

## Source discipline

The wiki should never rely on abstract summaries, secondary reviews, or casual plausibility. A detail only enters the system if it is traceable to a source the researcher has read or a verified bibliographic record.

## Practical rule

If a citation is missing, uncertain, or weak, do not improvise. Either verify it properly or delete the sentence.
